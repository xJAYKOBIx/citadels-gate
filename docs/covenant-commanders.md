# Covenant Commanders — character and art direction

Working document for the three Covenant commanders: who they are, how they talk, and how to get concept art made for them. The Forsaken three get the same treatment once these have landed. Stats stay in the Character Sheet; this doc is personality and look only.

## How the three fit together

The Covenant is an alliance, not a nation. Holts commands the human shieldwall that holds the line, Kyndrili leads the elven aerie that strikes behind it, Borvik builds the dwarven engines that knock down what the other two can't reach. They respect each other and don't entirely like each other, and that is the fun of their barks: Holts thinks Kyndrili takes risks with other people's soldiers, Kyndrili thinks Holts mistakes patience for wisdom, Borvik thinks both of them would be out of a job if they let him finish his cannon.

Shared visual rules so the three read as one faction: Covenant blue (#2f6fd6) as the dominant cloth or lacquer colour, old gold (#e2b54a) for trim, heraldry and light sources, pale steel for armour, warm skin and natural hair (no glowing eyes, no purple; that's the Forsaken). Every portrait is a head-and-shoulders bust, three-quarter view, eyes toward the viewer, against a soft abstract background in the faction colours. No weapons raised, no action poses: these are the faces on the commander cards and the match-setup screen, and they need to read at 64 px.

## Sir Asher Holts — Lord Marshal of the Shieldwall

**In one line:** the man who has never once been the most talented person in the room and has outlasted all of them.

**Personality.** Holts is in his early fifties, a career soldier from a minor house whose only inheritance was a sword and a sense of obligation. He is patient to a fault, dry rather than humourless, and allergic to speeches; his orders are short because he believes anything that needs explaining in battle has been planned badly. He trusts the shieldwall because he has stood in it. His flaw is caution: he will hold a position long after the moment to advance has passed, and he knows it, which is why he keeps Kyndrili close even though she exasperates him. He wants to bring his people home, in that order of priority, and he counts every one he doesn't.

**Voice.** Low, level, the voice of a man who has given these orders a thousand times. Never shouts. Dry understatement is his only joke.

Deploy: "Shields up. Hold the line." / "Sworn Guard, forward." / "Steady."
Rank up: "Good. Now we can do this properly."
Enemy elite sighted: "Something big on the left. Nobody panic."
Strike called: "Judgment. Clear the ground."
Tower lost: "We'll have that back."
Winning: "That's the gate. Finish it cleanly."
Victory: "Well held. Count the wounded."
Defeat: "Fall back to the road. We're not done; we're just done here."

**Visual direction.** Broad, weathered, a soldier's face rather than a lord's: heavy brow, close-cropped grey-brown hair and beard, an old scar through one eyebrow, tired eyes that are nonetheless paying attention. Plate armour with a blue surcoat worn almost to grey at the shoulders, a gold shieldwall emblem (three interlocked shields) on the breast, a plain wool cloak. Nothing ornate on him: the gold is all in the heraldry, not the man. Silhouette cue: squared shoulders, pauldrons like a wall.

## Kyndrili Veli'sha — Windsinger of the Silver Aerie

**In one line:** she sees the whole battlefield from above and finds the people on the ground a little slow.

**Personality.** Kyndrili is old by human measure and young by elven, which she uses as an excuse in both directions. She is serene on the surface, restless underneath, and genuinely delighted by a clever flanking move, hers or the enemy's. She talks to the wind, and not entirely as a figure of speech: the Windsingers read the air currents the Moonhawks ride, and she narrates them under her breath. Her flaw is that she treats risk as a puzzle and forgets it has a body count; she has lost riders proving a point and it has not made her careful, only quieter afterwards. She wants the aerie to matter, to be more than the Covenant's scouts. She likes Holts more than she lets on and would rather die than say so.

**Voice.** Light, musical, slightly amused, with pauses in odd places as if listening to something else at the same time. Rarely gives direct orders; she makes observations that happen to be orders.

Deploy: "The wind favours this. Go." / "Moonhawks, up." / "Quietly now."
Rank up: "Ah. There is the sky I wanted."
Enemy elite sighted: "Something heavy and slow. How thoughtful of them."
Strike called: "Look up."
Tower lost: "A tower is only a place to stand. We'll stand elsewhere."
Winning: "Their gate is open. I can see straight through."
Victory: "The wind told me. I merely agreed."
Defeat: "The air went still. Next time I will listen sooner."

**Visual direction.** Long, angular, luminous: silver-white hair worn in a high braid that the wind has pulled loose on one side, pale skin with a faint cool undertone, grey-green eyes, pointed ears with small silver rings. Light scale armour in blue-lacquered leather over silver mail, a feathered mantle in white and grey (Moonhawk plumage) across one shoulder, a slim gold circlet. Expression: a half-smile that is not quite kind. Silhouette cue: the asymmetric feather mantle and the braid.

## Thane Borvik Stonehallow — Master of Engines

**In one line:** the happiest man on any battlefield, because the battlefield is where the big machines go.

**Personality.** Borvik is a thane in the dwarven sense, which is to say he holds a hall and a seat, and he has spent his life trying to be in the workshop instead of the seat. He is loud, cheerful, profane in the gentle way of someone who swears at bolts rather than people, and he loves his engines with an affection he has never quite managed for people, though he tries. He names every cannon. His flaw is impatience disguised as confidence: he will fire before the sappers are clear because the shot is *right there*, and he has a Sapper Wagon crew who will tell you about it. He wants to build something that outlasts him and is privately afraid none of it will. He thinks Holts is sound and Kyndrili is a show-off, and he would take a Thunder Cannon shot for either of them.

**Voice.** Booming, gravelly, fast, full of asides to the machines. The only commander who sounds like he is enjoying himself.

Deploy: "Mind the wheels!" / "Right, lass, sing for them." (to the cannon) / "Sappers up, and this time wait for my signal."
Rank up: "Now *that* is worth the coin. Bring out the big one."
Enemy elite sighted: "Oh, a big one. Oh, that's lovely. Load."
Strike called: "Everybody back from the mark. Further than that."
Tower lost: "Stone's cheap. Load."
Winning: "Their wall's coming down! Did you hear that? *Did you hear that?*"
Victory: "Ha! Write that on the barrel. No, the other barrel."
Defeat: "Well. She'll need a new axle. So will I."

**Visual direction.** Wide, barrel-chested, a face mostly beard: russet going grey, braided in two forks with iron rings, singed at the ends. Soot on the cheekbones, a leather apron over blue-enamelled plate, goggles pushed up onto a bald, freckled scalp, one eyebrow permanently higher than the other. A brass-and-gold engineer's chain of office that he clearly forgets he is wearing. Small burn scars on the hands. Expression: mid-grin, about to say something. Silhouette cue: goggles up, the forked beard, the apron straps.

## Concept art: three directions

Generate all three commanders in the same direction before choosing; the point is a set, not three good individual pictures. Each prompt below is written to paste into an image generator as-is; the bracketed character block is swapped per commander. Avoid naming any existing game, film or artist in the prompt so the result is ours.

**Shared suffix (append to every prompt):** head-and-shoulders portrait, three-quarter view, looking at the viewer, soft abstract background in deep blue and old gold, faction colours royal blue and antique gold, no text, no watermark, no weapon raised, centred composition with headroom.

### Direction A — painted fantasy portrait

The safest and most "premium" look: oil-painting realism, visible brushwork, dramatic rim light in gold. Reads well at card size because the faces are modelled with strong value contrast.

`Oil painting portrait, fantasy realism, confident brushwork, warm golden rim light against cool blue shadow, textured canvas, [CHARACTER BLOCK], ` + shared suffix

### Direction B — graphic card art

Flat-shaded, strong clean shapes, two or three tones per surface, crisp line work. Matches the low-poly battlefield best and is the most legible on a phone. Slight risk of looking like a mobile-game cliché; keep the faces characterful, not cute.

`Stylised digital illustration, flat shading with hard-edged shadow shapes, limited palette, clean confident linework, slightly exaggerated features, painterly texture only in the background, [CHARACTER BLOCK], ` + shared suffix

### Direction C — illuminated manuscript

Woodcut-and-gilt look: heavy ink line, parchment ground, gold leaf accents, muted mineral pigments. The most distinctive and the best fit for the "Grimoire" framing of the card deck. Hardest to make the faces expressive; may need more re-rolls.

`Illuminated manuscript portrait, woodcut ink lines, gold leaf accents, parchment texture, muted mineral pigments with royal blue lapis, medieval illustration style with modern draughtsmanship, [CHARACTER BLOCK], ` + shared suffix

### Character blocks

**Holts:** `a weathered human knight in his fifties, heavy brow, close-cropped grey-brown hair and beard, old scar through one eyebrow, tired attentive eyes, plate armour under a faded blue surcoat with a gold emblem of three interlocked shields, plain wool cloak, squared shoulders, expression calm and dry`

**Kyndrili:** `an elegant elven woman, long angular face, silver-white hair in a high braid pulled loose by wind on one side, pale skin with a cool undertone, grey-green eyes, pointed ears with small silver rings, blue-lacquered leather scale armour over silver mail, a feathered white-and-grey mantle over one shoulder, slim gold circlet, expression a faint knowing half-smile`

**Borvik:** `a broad dwarven engineer, bald freckled scalp with brass goggles pushed up on it, russet beard going grey braided into two forks with iron rings, singed beard ends, soot on the cheekbones, leather apron over blue-enamelled plate, a brass chain of office, small burn scars on the hands, one eyebrow raised, expression a wide delighted grin mid-sentence`

### Practical notes

Ask for 1:1 for the commander cards and 2:3 for the match-setup panel; the same image can be cropped from the 2:3 if the headroom is there. Generate four of each, pick by whether the face reads at thumbnail size (shrink the browser tab; if the expression survives, it's a keeper). Once a direction is chosen, the Forsaken three get the same shared suffix with the colours swapped to crimson and bone (#8e2630 / #e3d6b8).

The in-game crests stay as fallbacks until the portraits are in; dropping a 512×512 PNG per commander into `art/portraits/` and pointing the card at it is a ten-line change when we get there.
